Plants Lab

Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 · Topic 6 plant nutrition, Topic 8 transport in plants, 14.5 tropic responses, 16.3 sexual reproduction in plants, 18.2 adaptive features

In one minute

  • Click any part of the plant, or any step on the bar along the bottom. The stations run from the seed under the soil to the fruit at the top, and the plant lights the part each one is about.
  • Each station has two tabs: Learn — what you must know, with something to press, drag or watch in every section — and Practise, the questions.
  • Check as often as you like. You are told which parts are wrong, never the answer — you have to work it out. Keep going until it is all right, then Hand in.
  • An underlined word can be clicked: 🔍 opens a picture there and then, ↗ jumps to the station that explains it and brings you back, and a faint dotted line opens the definition.
  • Your work is saved in this browser on this device. Use the same computer each time, and hand in before you clear your browsing data.

The answers are never shown. To hand your work to Dr Mompel you sign in with your school Google account, so he knows whose it is; anyone else is welcome to use the lab and will still get a completion code.

What the colours mean
  • Blue words are about water and transport — xylem, root hairs, transpiration. Amber words are photosynthesis and the food it makes — chlorophyll, glucose, phloem, sucrose. Rose words are reproduction — the parts of a flower, pollen, seed, fruit. Violet words are growth and adaptation — tropisms, auxin, xerophytes.
  • The plant on the left lights in the same colours: blue sap running up the stem is water in the xylem, amber sap is sugar in the phloem.
  • A green-bordered box is the mistake students most often make there. A small S badge means Supplement (Paper 4 only). “Beyond the syllabus” is worth knowing but not named in 0610.
  • The colour key sits at the top of every Learn tab, so you never have to remember.
What is simplified here
  • The plant is a bean — a dicotyledon — because that is the plant the syllabus sections are drawn from. A grass or a conifer would look different inside.
  • The interactive graphs are models with the exam’s shapes, not measurements from a real leaf.
  • The pollen tube animation shows one nucleus fusing with one; the second fusion real flowering plants make (the endosperm) is left out, as the syllabus says.
  • The cactus and the water lily stand for all xerophytes and hydrophytes; the syllabus asks for the features, not the species.
